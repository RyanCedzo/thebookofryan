## What is Happiness?

It's a question that has defined much of my life. This ever-escaping concept that I strive to not only experience, but also define.
Such a simple yet complicated idea that constantly knocks at the doors of my brain. Continuing with that analogy, I'd say happiness is a 
guest that proudly comes and goes as it pleases without much consideration for its host. However, an analogy is not a definition and I usually
find myself more confused the harder I try to define it. 

I will start these words with a warning - when this project is considered complete, my final thoughts will most likely define happiness in an over-simplified all-encompassing cliché statement, but
I think I already know I won't be able to define it any other way. 

## The Book of Happiness

I love projects. For someone that has always felt growing up that he was void of any creativity, I have a surprisingly large ambition for creating. I learned that 
creativity comes in so many forms, and I learned to use the creative outlets that best complemented my own strengths. Usually these projects include coding of some sort or approaching different aspects
of life from a fresh angle, striving to be different almost to a fault. This project, however, is relatively basic. As mentioned, I've spent a lot of my life searching for happiness. This search has come in
so many different forms - chasing academic passion, travel, relationships, buying and converting a van, and many of my personal projects. All of these chases were trying to catch the most basic form of happiness - 
a feeling. From a chemical perspective, I feel I have just been craving dopamine, seratonin, and oxytocin in some form of addiction. But that is just me, and like most things I do, I wanted to dig deeper and turn this
exploration into a larger project. I wanted something I could examine and learn. It was at this point that my book of happiness was born. A single notebook I could carry with me wherever I go that posed a single question - "What is Happiness?"
Over the past few months, I have taken this notebook across the United States from the east to west coast and even Hawai'i. The goal has been to fill the book with answers from friends, strangers, and everyone in-between. The hardest part so far
has been remembering to have people write in the book as I often forget until after leaving an interaction. Even so, I have still had roughly 20-25 entries into the book. This brings me to the purpose of this post. I wanted an
"initial thoughts" log before the project goes any further. Then, when I have filled up the book, I can look back on my early thoughts and see how the project has evolved.

## Initial Thoughts & Examples

Before going any further into my thoughts, I first wanted to mention the biggest hidden reason for this book. I love memories. As much as I try to live in the present, I find myself reminiscing in the past often. I suppose 
a big part of my goal of being present revolves around making memories. So many decisions in my life are made through the lense of the result becoming a good story/memory. At the same time, I travel and move around a lot. This 
is one part of my life I love, and I could spend hours talking about how this type of travel slows down life and inserts me right into moments. But one aspect of this type of travel that becomes difficult is always saying goodbye.
For every friend I make, it is usually a matter of time before I move onto my next place, unsure if I will ever see them again. Having an entire book with the happiest of memories and thoughts from those I got to share with just sounded
like a wonderful opportunity. So even though I will continue to talk about this book as an experiment, deep down it is a treasure that is slowly becoming one of my most prized possessions - a collection of happiness from all the differeing 
perspectives from those I love and beyond.

In my inital experiences I found that there were essentially 3 different routes someone would take when writing in the book. The first would be the experiental definition - defining happiness from a single experience (or sometimes a few). Here are some examples:

- "Watching the sunset"
- "Poetry! Movies! Books!...Feeling understood. Teaching and Learning. Love!"
- "Fireflies & campfires on a summer evening"
- "Gray fog rolling down a mountainside...the laughter of friends...sleeping in on a rainy day...walking barefoot on a soft patch of grass"
- "Being with people you don't have to put on an act to please...it is laughing so much your tummy aches and your cheeks are sore...finding a new favorite song and learning all the words"

I love reading these simple, honest, and straightforward answers. There are so many small experiences/sensations that can bring us joy. It's special to both experience these on our own, but also to be able to read about others' experiences and learn about all the small things that bring
happiness to different people. These are the types of experiences I like to think about when considering appreciating the small parts of life. Such small simple things can bring us so much joy.

The second type of approach some people would take when writing in the book is the contextual/relational definition - defining happiness in the context of shared experiences between me and that person. Here are some examples that are mostly fractions of much longer excerpts:

- "Happiness is waking up in a beach parking lot with a bunch of homies and a free public restroom"
- "Happiness is sweet friends singing together"
- "Happiness is listening to the birds sing their songs & trying to capture their fleeting moments on film"
- "Happiness is a good meal, late night Denny's pancakes, writing silly lyrics with friends, campfires, swimming, writing a nice love note on what happiness is..."
- "Happiness is flipping over rocks in the stream in search of salymanders"

This approach isn't directly defining happiness, but instead personalizing it in recent events with a person
that would later become memories. These are usually the excerpts I go back to read most often. It also makes sense that a lot of entries take this approach, as most people I have writing in my book are people I meet as I travel, sometimes briefly with only a few shared experiences, other times
with friends I have known for a long time. Even though this approach is only defining happiness in a very specific context, it doesn't mean that these entries are any less relative to the defining of happiness. I think that this approach argues that happiness many times is derived directly through shared experiences.

The final approach is the conceptual/abstract definition - defining happiness as a concept in a unique abstract way. Here are a few examples:

- "The moment your brain is completely blank and you fully emerge in the moment"
- "Doing what you love and doing it honestly"
- "Finding your own path instead of submitting to what's expected of you"
- "Happiness is loving and being loved"
- "Happiness is not one simple emotion. It is not something you feel independently. You may be happy, sad, excited, regretful, or many other things at the same time. Feeling them all is being human. It sucks & it is great"

In terms of defining happiness, this approach is the most direct. I have gone back to many of these thoughts when I felt like I needed advice. I am lucky to be surrounded by people so full of wisdom and wonderful philosophies. I find it
incredible that there can be all different types of definitions yet I agree with so many of them. I think that is proof as to why it is so difficult to define. 

I don't see one approach as any more correct than the others. I think that together, they form a very cohesive definition and this leads me to my first definition.

## So What is Happiness?

When I first set out to write this initial consolidation of ideas, one of the main goals was to have some sort of definition of happiness by the end of it. I think it is a bit of a cop out that I am essentially calling this an "initial definition"
with the promise that my definition will change over time. For the most part, I'm taking the easy route that will allow me to change my answer later. Anyways, I first started writing this post last night when it was very late. Around 2AM is when I 
decided I would take a break and finish it the next day. Around 2:30 in the morning, before I had fallen asleep, some thoughts struck me that I jotted down into my notes to read in the morning. I think that these thoughts are the closest I will get to 
a definition to start - **Happiness is a feeling of purpose and belonging**. I chose to include both purpose and belonging to cover happiness as a shared feeling, but also as an individual experience. Purpose covers happiness we can feel alone. It is
the feeling and understanding that we are living a life according to our values and ideals. It includes all the times we are proud of ourselves for our accomplishments and choices. It includes all the decisions that make us who we are. In addition to this, 
belonging is more of a communal feeling. The feeling that we are exactly where we need to be. Shared experiences with friends and strangers can create that sense of belonging. I usually feel the greatest senses of belonging
when sharing a laugh or a smile. Through both of these feelings - purpose and belonging - we feel that sense of fullfillment that I call happiness. 

## Final Notes

I just wanted to also note, that this is the beginning of the journey. I hope that by the end, I will have changed my perspective a multitude of times and come across all sorts of new ideas and experiences. I also can't help but mention the feeling of dishonestly
as I write this. I feel like deep down happiness cannot be defined at all and trying to define it only lessens it's impact. Sure, this may even be true, but I think that I will learn enough on this journey that it will make it worth it. There's a song I love - Gates by The Menzingers
with the lyrics "You'll carve your names into the Paupack cliffs just to read them when you get old enough to know that happiness is just a moment". Sometimes, I feel like that might be the best definition of happiness - a moment. That word has been pestering me a 
lot in the past few months, how our life is just a collection of moments. Some we remember and others are lost forever. Maybe when I get older I'll think the same things. Only time will tell I guess. Regardless,
I'm excited to keep this project going and I absolutely love my book of happiness as a personal object of its own. I hope if anyone reads these words that they have either already written in the book, or do some day. Oof, that was much longer than I had originally planned.
